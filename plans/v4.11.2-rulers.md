# v4.11.2: rulers in races

**Goal:** an object beside a ruler, read in centimeters, then inches, then compared, as the third picture track beside blocks and money ([spec 11](specs/11-new-ways-to-answer.md#picture-math)).

## The admin's calls (2026-11-17)

- **Measuring:** an object beside a ruler, read in centimeters, then in inches; later, comparing ("which is longer, 10 cm or 10 inches?").
- **One game:** a picture track inside races, chosen by need like blocks and money (v4.11.1).
- **A two-sided ruler, as in real life:** centimeters on the top edge and inches on the bottom, so converting between them and seeing the difference is reinforced every time.

## My calls

- **A third track, `ruler`,** in `pictures.py`; the neediest track gets a race's picture slot, as now.
- **Drawn by code as an SVG:** a two-sided ruler (every centimeter numbered on top; every inch numbered with half-inch marks below), and above it a colored bar with an interest's emoji at its end (🦕, 🏎️), or a pencil when none matches. A dashed line drops from the bar's end across both edges. The unit names (cm, in) sit to the left of the ruler, beside each row of numbers.
- **One scale for both edges:** 22 px per centimeter, so an inch (2.54 cm) is 56 px, its real size beside a centimeter: a 12 cm ruler shows about 4¾ inches, and fits a phone.
- **Levels** (moving every 5 answers):
  1. the centimeter edge, the object starting at 0, whole numbers (2–12): "How many centimeters long is the dinosaur?";
  2. the inch edge, starting at 0, whole numbers (1–4);
  3. centimeters, the object starting past 0 ("from 3 to 9"): the length is the difference;
  4. inches with halves (1½, 2½): typed "1.5" or "1 1/2";
  5. converting: "The dinosaur is 3 inches long. How many centimeters, to the nearest one?" (read on the other edge: 8);
  6. comparing: two objects on the same ruler, one given in centimeters and one in inches ("8 cm or 3 inches: which is longer? Type 1 or 2"), lengths at least 1 cm apart.
- **Answers are numbers** on the decimal keyboard; lengths are kept in halves in code (never floats), so 1.5 and "1 1/2" are the same answer.
- **After an answer:** how to read it ("It starts at 3 and ends at 9: 6 cm."), and for inches, conversion, and comparing, the link: "An inch is about 2½ cm."

## Tests (fakes only, minimal)

- **Happy path:** each level's answer matches its drawing (length = end − start; the nearest centimeter at level 5; the longer one at level 6, by real size); `length_typed` reads "1.5", "1 1/2", and "2", and refuses "1/0".
