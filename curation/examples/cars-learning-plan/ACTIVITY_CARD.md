# Activity card: what does 0–60 tell us?

An example, filled in. It is for row 4 of the [example plan](LEARNING_PLAN.csv), because that activity already exists as a draft in the app. It is not approved.

**Interest:** cars
**Goal for the whole plan:** tell apart distance, time, average speed, and change in speed
**This activity (row number on your plan sheet):** 4, acceleration, starting from average speed

## What the student sees

**Short explanation before the question (one to three screens):**

1. A car card might say 0–60 in 4 seconds. The 4 seconds are how long the speed change took.
2. Acceleration means how quickly speed changes. If two cars make the same 0–60 change, the car with the shorter time has greater average acceleration.

**Question:** Car A goes from 0 to 60 in 4 seconds. Car B takes 8 seconds. Which car has greater average acceleration?

**Choices (two or three):**

- Car A — 4 seconds
- Car B — 8 seconds
- They have the same average acceleration

**Right answer:** Car A

**Hints, in order (one or two):**

1. Both cars make the same speed change: 0 to 60 miles per hour.
2. The same change in less time means greater average acceleration.

**If they get it right, say:** Yes. Car A makes the same speed change in less time, so its average acceleration is greater.

**If the hints run out, say:** It is Car A. Both cars go from 0 to 60, but Car A does it in less time.

**Picture idea:** two cars side by side, each with its 0–60 time

**What the picture shows, in words (for anyone who can't see it):** Two cars both go from 0 to 60 miles per hour. Car A takes 4 seconds and Car B takes 8 seconds.

## For you

**How you'll know they really get it** (not just picked the right choice): they can say why Car A wins (same change, less time), not just tap it.

**Where the facts come from** (a book or website you trust): NASA Glenn Research Center, [Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/WWW/K-12/airplane/disvelac.html)

**Anything to avoid or be careful about:** 0–60 shows an average; don't say the car speeds up evenly the whole time.
